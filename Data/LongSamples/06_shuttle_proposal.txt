Campus Shuttle Service Improvement Proposal

The campus transport committee is reviewing the evening shuttle service between the residence halls, the library, the academic buildings, and the main gate. Students report that the existing timetable works reasonably well during ordinary teaching hours but becomes unreliable after laboratory sessions and society meetings. The proposed change is to increase departures during the evening peak while keeping a less frequent service during quiet periods. The objective is to reduce waiting without operating nearly empty vehicles throughout the day.

Before changing the route, the committee will collect passenger counts at each stop for two ordinary teaching weeks. Volunteers will record the scheduled departure, actual arrival, number of people boarding, and number left behind. They will distinguish delays caused by traffic at the gate from delays caused by long boarding queues. Personal names and student identification numbers are unnecessary for this study. A short survey will ask passengers about their usual destination, acceptable waiting time, and difficulty reaching the existing stops.

The proposed route begins near the residence halls, passes the library, continues to the laboratory complex, and returns through the academic square. A separate connection to the main gate would run at selected times rather than on every circuit. This arrangement could shorten the central loop, but it might inconvenience passengers travelling outside campus. The committee therefore wants to compare the current route with the proposed route using actual journey times rather than assuming that a shorter map distance always means a faster service.

Accessibility is another concern. Some stops have uneven pavement, poor lighting, or no shelter during heavy rain. A reliable timetable alone will not solve these problems. The proposal includes clearly marked boarding areas, visible route information, and a way to request assistance. Drivers should avoid moving the shuttle until boarding is complete. Any change to stop locations should be reviewed with passengers who have difficulty walking long distances or climbing steps.

The operating budget must include driver hours, vehicle maintenance, fuel or charging costs, and reserve capacity when a shuttle is unavailable. Increasing the number of departures could improve convenience but also raise expenses. The committee will first test a revised timetable using the existing fleet. If passenger queues remain long, a later proposal may consider additional vehicles. No purchase decision should be based only on a single crowded evening.

During the trial, weekly summaries will compare waiting times, punctuality, passenger counts, and missed connections. Feedback will be collected from drivers as well as students. A successful trial should reduce the longest waits while maintaining safe boarding and a manageable workload. The final recommendation will explain which parts of the timetable should continue, which stops need improvement, and whether the main gate connection requires a separate service.
