Student Feedback on the Evening Shuttle Trial

The student transport group reviewed feedback from passengers using the evening shuttle between the residence halls, library, laboratory complex, and main gate. Many respondents welcomed the more regular departures near the end of the teaching day. However, several reported that the published timetable did not match the time the vehicle actually reached their stop. The group recommends displaying expected arrival information separately from the departure time at the beginning of the route.

Crowding was most noticeable after laboratory sessions ended. At the academic square, passengers sometimes formed two queues because the boarding area was not clearly marked. Students waiting at a later stop occasionally found that the shuttle was already full. Adding a departure immediately after a large class might help, but the group does not recommend increasing service uniformly across the entire evening. Passenger demand changes considerably between teaching days, examination periods, and holidays.

The shorter central route made journeys between the library and residence halls easier. On the other hand, students travelling through the main gate disliked changing vehicles or waiting for a separate connection. Some respondents preferred the old circular route even though it took longer. The report therefore separates two needs: frequent short journeys within campus and less frequent journeys to the gate. A timetable that serves one group well may still perform poorly for the other.

Several comments concerned the condition of the stops rather than the vehicles. Passengers described dark waiting areas, puddles near the pavement, and difficulty reading route information during rain. Students carrying equipment from the laboratories asked for enough time to board safely. Passengers with reduced mobility requested more accessible boarding locations. These issues should be included in the transport review instead of treating average waiting time as the only measure of service quality.

Drivers explained that delays sometimes accumulated when a vehicle waited for a connecting service or when traffic blocked the gate. They also noted that breaks and vehicle inspections must remain part of the operating timetable. The student group supports recording these causes separately. A late arrival caused by a safety inspection should not be interpreted in the same way as a departure missed because the timetable was unrealistic.

For the next trial, the group proposes collecting passenger counts, actual arrival times, and the number of people unable to board. A short weekly survey could identify persistent problems without requiring students to submit personal travel histories. The final comparison should include the longest waits, not just the average. The recommendation is to retain the central shuttle route provisionally, improve stop lighting and signs, and revise the main gate connection after another period of observation.
