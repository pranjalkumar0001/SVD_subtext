Workflow for Cataloguing the Digital Library Archive

The archive team needs a consistent procedure for turning scanned college publications into reliable catalogue records. This workflow begins when a librarian selects a physical item and assigns a stable identifier. That identifier must appear in the image filenames, the catalogue entry, and the record of any later corrections. Keeping these links consistent prevents a search result from opening the wrong magazine or pointing to a page from another publication.

Before scanning, a volunteer records the title, date if available, creator, and document type. The volunteer also checks the condition of the paper and notes any missing pages. Unknown information is left blank with an explanatory note. Dates should be taken from the publication itself when possible. Handwritten annotations on a storage folder may be useful evidence, but they should not automatically override the printed source. A second reviewer checks entries that contain uncertain descriptions.

After scanning, the team inspects the images for blurred text, cropped margins, incorrect rotation, and missing pages. Files that fail inspection are returned for rescanning where the physical condition allows it. Optical character recognition is then used to obtain searchable text. The output often contains mistakes in names, headings, and articles arranged in several columns. Volunteers correct these errors by comparing the recognised text with the page image rather than relying on what they expect the sentence to say.

The catalogue uses a small subject vocabulary to keep descriptions consistent. For example, different volunteers should not assign unrelated labels to records about the same annual event. Nevertheless, the original title and article wording are retained. Subject labels help readers browse the collection, while the full text supports more specific searches. Every correction should include a short explanation and a record of which version was changed, so that accidental edits can be investigated.

Public access is reviewed independently of scanning quality. A clear image may still contain personal contact information, private correspondence, or photographs whose publication permission is uncertain. The librarian records the access decision in the catalogue. Restricted items may remain visible as catalogue descriptions without exposing their full contents. Copies prepared for public release should be kept separate from preservation copies when redaction is required.

The final review uses search tasks rather than simply counting completed scans. A reviewer searches for a known article, checks whether the correct record appears, opens the linked pages, and verifies the title and date. Failed searches are investigated for missing metadata, inconsistent subject labels, or recognition errors. The team also checks that backup copies can be restored. The workflow is considered usable only when records can be found, interpreted, corrected, and connected reliably to their original documents.
